<b>Title</b>: Understanding the Limitations of Differentiable Reverbs
-----------------

<b>Abstract</b>: Differentiable digital signal processing promised us the best of both worlds: the efficiency and interpretability of classic audio algorithms, tuned with the same gradient-based optimizers used to train neural networks. Feedback delay networks (FDNs), a popular structure for efficient artificial reverberation, are a natural candidate. In practice, however, making a differentiable FDN match a real room depends less on the optimizer than on the model. In this talk, I will share what I learned while building optimization pipelines for FDNs, and where they failed. I will argue that much of the difficulty comes from assumptions about the target acoustics, built into the model and the loss, that do not hold in practice, such as a single, homogeneous decay or a noise-free measurement. More broadly, I will discuss which parts of the problem are best handled by an informed design and which are better left to a neural network.

<b>Bio</b>: Gloria Dal Santo is a doctoral student at the Acoustics Lab in the Department of Information and Communications Engineering at Aalto University, Finland. She received her M.Sc. degree in electrical and electronic engineering from EPFL, Switzerland, in 2022. Her research interests include artificial reverberation and the application of machine learning and differentiable digital signal processing to digital audio effects.
