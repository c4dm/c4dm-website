C4DM PhD student [Christos Plachouras](https://chrispla.me/) has been awarded a prestigious Google PhD Fellowship in Machine Perception, in recognition of his pioneering research in audio AI.

The fellowship supports exceptional doctoral students conducting innovative research in computer science and related fields, providing funding and opportunities to engage with leading researchers across academia and industry.


## Expanding the Frontiers of Audio AI

Christos Plachouras' research explores how machines can listen to, understand and explain sound using natural language.

Many sectors already rely on automated sound analysis, from biodiversity monitoring in environmental conservation to music recommendation and organisation in the creative industries. However, despite recent advances in artificial intelligence, enabling machines to understand complex audio remains a significant challenge.

Christos' work addresses this challenge by developing improved methods for representing information from sound, generating synthetic training data and evaluating how AI systems interpret audio. His research aims to help create more capable and transparent AI systems that can understand sound beyond human speech. He said:

“I’m delighted to receive the Google PhD Fellowship and excited for the opportunity to advance my research into how machines understand sound. I’m especially grateful to my supervisors and everyone who has supported me for their guidance, encouragement, and trust.”


## Recognising the Next Generation of Researchers

Commenting on this year's fellows, Leslie Yeh, Director of Scientific Progress at Google.org, said:

“Every major leap in technology starts with a researcher asking a bold question long before the world sees the answer.

The 2026 Google PhD Fellows are asking those questions today. With funding and a global community behind them, exceptional early-career scientists like Christos Plachouras at Queen Mary University of London have the freedom to pursue ambitious ideas and help build a stronger, more representative scientific landscape for everyone.”

The awards highlight the strength of doctoral research within EECS and recognise the global impact of research taking place across fields including healthcare AI, machine learning and audio intelligence.

You can take a look the complete list of fellowship recipients on [Google's website](https://research.google/programs-and-events/phd-fellowship/recipients/).
